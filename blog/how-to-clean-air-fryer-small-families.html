Your Air Fryer Is Dirtier Than You Think

The air fryer is the hardest-working gadget in most small kitchens. It crisps the fries, roasts the chicken, reheats the leftovers — and every single cook leaves a little grease behind. That grease does not just sit there. The next time the fan blows hot air over it, the grease bakes on a little harder, and the time after that a little harder still. After a few weeks of "I will clean it tomorrow," the basket is sticky, the food starts to smoke, and the fries taste like last week's fish.

Here is the part most people learn too late: air fryers do not usually die from cooking. They die from cleaning — steel wool, oven spray, and a dunked base unit. Most baskets use a thin PTFE or ceramic nonstick coating, and the manuals from Ninja, Philips, and COSORI all warn the same way: scrub that coating once with something rough and it is scratched for good. Food sticks harder, grease builds faster, and the coating starts to flake. This guide shows the gentle way to clean every part of an air fryer, how often to do it, and the signs that yours is past saving.

What to Look For: 5 Signs Your Air Fryer Needs a Deep Clean

Smoke every time you cook. Grease pooled in the bottom of the drawer hits the heating element and smokes. Philips' own manual says it plainly: remove oil and fat from the bottom of the pan after every use to prevent smoke.

Food sticks where it never used to. A basket that once released fries cleanly and now grips them has a worn or grease-filmed coating. A deep clean fixes the film; only a new basket fixes a scratched coating.

A smell that survives washing. Old grease and spice residue bake into the coating. If a normal wash does not fix it, the vinegar wipe in the FAQ below is the next step.

Crumbs rattling under the basket. Crumbs fall through the holes and collect in the drawer. Left there, they burn on the next cook and add a bitter taste.

Grease on the heating element. Tilt the unit gently and look up inside. Splatter from fatty foods — bacon, sausages, chicken skin — lands on the element and bakes into a brown crust.

What You Need (3 Things)

You do not need a special air fryer cleaner. Warm water, mild dish soap, and a soft non-abrasive sponge handle the everyday clean. For baked-on grease, add baking soda — that is the full toolkit. A soft brush helps for the heating element, and a dry towel finishes the job. Skip steel wool, metal scrapers, harsh scouring powder, oven cleaner, and bleach. They all damage the nonstick coating, and oven cleaner is too strong for the plastic outer shell as well.

How to Clean an Air Fryer After Every Cook (2 Minutes)

First, always unplug the air fryer and let it cool for at least 30 minutes. Rinsing a hot basket under cold water causes thermal shock — a fast temperature swing that stresses the nonstick layer. While it cools, tip the drawer over a container and scrape out the fat and crumbs; do not pour fat down the sink.

Then wash the basket and tray in warm water with a few drops of mild dish soap, using a soft sponge. Rinse, dry fully, and slide the basket back in. That two-minute habit after every cook is what stops grease from ever becoming a deep-clean job — cleaning experts say a same-day hand wash prevents nearly all serious buildup. The Weekly Deep Clean, Step by Step

If you use the air fryer daily, plan a full clean about once a week. It takes around 20 minutes of work, plus soaking time.

Step 1 — Unplug and cool. Always unplug first and let the unit cool completely before cleaning.

Step 2 — Empty the fat. Tip the drawer over a heat-safe container and scrape out rendered fat and crumbs before anything touches water.

Step 3 — Soak the stuck stuff. Fill the sink or a basin with hot water and a few drops of dish soap, then set the basket and tray in to soak for 10 to 15 minutes. That window comes straight from the Philips and COSORI manuals. For heavy grease, soak up to an hour — but do not soak longer than 30 minutes if your basket has a ceramic coating, as long water contact can stress it.

Step 4 — Wash with a soft sponge. Hot water, dish soap, a soft sponge. Rinse well and dry completely. If grease is still stuck, spread a paste of 2 parts baking soda to 1 part water over the spots, leave it for 15 minutes, and wipe gently with the sponge.

Step 5 — Wipe the inside. Wipe the inside walls of the unit with a damp cloth. Never pour water into the base unit — it holds the electrics, and every manual forbids submerging it.

How to Clean the Heating Element and the Outside

Flip the unit gently or tilt it so you can see the heating element, and flick food residue off with a soft brush. Never spray water or liquid cleaner directly on the element — it is an electrical part, not a surface to scrub. For the outside, a damp cloth with a drop of dish soap is enough; dry it right away so no streaks stay on the plastic. And if you are using an oil sprayer for your air fryer, wipe the nozzle too — a clogged sprayer is the reason most people over-oil, and over-oiling is the reason baskets get greasy in the first place.

What NOT to Do — 5 Mistakes That Ruin the Basket

Steel wool or abrasive pads. One pass takes the coating off. Ninja's manual says it plainly: those tools "may damage the nonstick coating," and the damage does not heal.

Soaking the base unit. Only the removable basket and tray should ever touch water. The main unit, cord, and plug must stay dry.

Oven cleaner or bleach. Both etch the coating and are too harsh for the plastic shell. Skip them entirely.

Spraying the heating element. No water, no spray, no liquid cleaner on the element — a soft brush only.

Cleaning while it is still hot. A hot basket under cold water can warp the coating. Wait until it is cool to the touch.

How Often Should You Clean an Air Fryer?

Quick wash after every cook, deep clean once a week if you use it daily, and a heating-element check once a month. That is the whole schedule. A small family that air-fries three or four times a week can stretch the deep clean to every two weeks, but the after-every-cook wipe is not optional — grease left until the next day is far harder to remove, and COSORI specifically flags fatty foods like bacon and sausages as the worst offenders. Hand Washing vs Dishwasher: Pros & Cons

✅ Hand Washing

  • Gentle on the nonstick coating — lasts years longer
  • Takes about 90 seconds after a normal cook
  • You see the grease you missed and catch it early
  • No harsh dishwasher detergent on the coating
  • Works for every basket, dishwasher-safe or not

❌ Dishwasher

  • Heat and strong detergent wear the coating down
  • Food can bake on during the heated dry cycle
  • Only the top rack is safe — easy to load wrong
  • Some baskets are not dishwasher-safe at all
  • Saves 2 minutes but can cost you a whole basket

Final Verdict

Cleaning an air fryer is a two-minute habit, not a weekend project — wash the basket while it is still warm, soak it for 10 to 15 minutes when grease bakes on, use a baking soda paste for the stubborn spots, and never let steel wool, oven cleaner, or water near the parts that should stay dry. Do that, and the basket that came with your air fryer will outlast the air fryer itself. FAQ

Can I put my air fryer basket in the dishwasher?

Many baskets are labelled dishwasher-safe, but manufacturers and cleaning experts say hand washing makes the nonstick coating last longer — heat and strong dishwasher detergent wear it down over time. If you do use the dishwasher, put the basket on the top rack and skip the heated dry.

Can I use vinegar or baking soda to clean an air fryer?

Yes, both are safe when used the right way. Spread a baking soda paste (2 parts baking soda to 1 part water) on stuck grease, leave it for 15 minutes, then wipe it off with a soft sponge. A splash of vinegar in the soak water also helps cut grease. Do not mix vinegar and baking soda together — the fizz cancels out the cleaning power of both.

How do I get the smell out of my air fryer?

Wash the basket and tray with warm soapy water, then wipe the inside with a cloth dipped in equal parts white vinegar and water. For strong smells from fish or spices, rub a cut lemon over the basket and leave it for a few minutes before washing. Always dry the basket fully before you store it — damp storage is what keeps smells alive.
