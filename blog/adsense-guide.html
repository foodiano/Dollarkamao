How Websites Earn Money with AdSense

A Beginner's Guide

By Talha · October 2026 · 7 min read

The Question Every Beginner Asks

When I started learning web development, I kept wondering the same thing: the blogs and tutorial sites I read every day are free — so how do they pay their bills? The answer, for millions of websites, is Google AdSense. It is the most popular way for small and medium websites to earn money, and it is free to join. But most beginners misunderstand how it works, how much it pays, and what it takes to get accepted. This guide covers all three, honestly.

If you are building your first website — like I did with Foodiano World — understanding AdSense early helps you make better decisions about your content, your site structure, and your expectations.

What Is Google AdSense?

Google AdSense is an advertising program run by Google. Website owners (called publishers) place ad units on their pages, and Google fills those units with ads from its huge network of advertisers. When visitors see or click those ads, Google pays the publisher a share of what the advertiser paid.

Think of it like renting out billboard space on your website. You own the land (your content and traffic), Google acts as the agency that finds advertisers and handles all the technical work, and you collect rent. You never have to find advertisers yourself, negotiate prices, or build an ad system — Google does all of that. Your only job is to create content that brings visitors.

AdSense is different from affiliate marketing, the other common earning method. With affiliates, you recommend specific products and earn a commission per sale. With AdSense, you simply show ads and earn from views and clicks. Many successful sites use both at the same time.

How AdSense Works, Step by Step

Step 1: You apply. You submit your website to Google for review. A reviewer (human or automated) checks that your site has original, substantial content, a professional structure, and complies with program policies. This is the gate most beginners worry about — I will cover exactly how to pass it below.

Step 2: You add the ad code. Once approved, Google gives you a small piece of code to paste into your pages. Modern AdSense mostly uses Auto Ads, where you paste one code snippet and Google automatically decides where ads look best on each page.

Step 3: Google runs an auction. Every time someone opens your page, advertisers bid in real time for the ad space. The highest bidder's ad is shown. This all happens in milliseconds, before the page finishes loading.

Step 4: You earn. You earn money in two ways: per click (CPC — cost per click), when a visitor clicks an ad, and per thousand views (RPM — revenue per mille), a smaller amount for ads simply being displayed. Google totals your earnings and pays you monthly once you cross the payment threshold (usually $100).

How Much Do You Earn Per Click?

This is the honest answer nobody likes: it depends — a lot. There is no fixed rate. A click can pay anywhere from a few cents to several dollars. What you earn depends on three main factors.

Your niche matters most. Advertisers in competitive industries (finance, insurance, software, legal services) bid high for clicks, so publishers in those niches earn more per click — sometimes $1 to $3 or more. Entertainment, memes, and general news niches earn far less per click, often just a few cents, because advertisers bid less there.

Your visitor's country matters. Clicks from countries with strong advertiser demand (like the US, UK, and Canada) generally pay more than clicks from countries with less ad spending. Two identical sites can earn very different amounts purely because of where their readers live.

Realistic numbers. As a rough guide, many content sites see an RPM (earnings per 1,000 page views) somewhere between $1 and $10. So 10,000 monthly page views might earn roughly $10 to $100. That is why traffic is everything: AdSense rewards sites with real, consistent readership, not tricks. Anyone promising you hundreds of dollars from a brand-new site with no visitors is not telling the truth.

3 Tips to Get Approved

Google rejects many first-time applications, but the reasons are almost always the same. Here are the three things that matter most.

Tip 1: Publish real, original content — enough of it. This is the number one reason for rejection: thin or copied content. Aim for multiple full-length, original articles (think 800+ words each) that genuinely help the reader. Write about what you actually know or are learning. My first article on this site was a 1,200-word guide about how I built Foodiano World — written from my own experience, not copied from anywhere. Google's reviewers can tell the difference between real writing and filler.

Tip 2: Make your site look complete and professional. Before applying, your site should feel like a finished product, not a work in progress. That means clear navigation, an About page explaining who you are, a Contact page so visitors can reach you, and a Privacy Policy disclosing cookies and advertising. Mobile-friendly design matters too, since most visitors arrive on phones. These pages signal that a real person runs a real site.

Tip 3: Follow the policies before you apply, not after. Read the AdSense program policies once, properly. The common traps: no copyrighted text or images you do not own, no misleading claims (especially about earning money), no encouraging people to click your ads, and no adult, violent, or hateful content. Also note that in some countries Google requires the domain to be at least six months old. A clean, policy-compliant site gets approved faster; a site that needs three reapplications wastes months.

What to Do After Approval

Getting approved is the starting line, not the finish line. After approval, your real job begins: publishing consistently, learning what your readers search for, and growing traffic month by month. Most sites earn very little in the first months — that is normal. The publishers who succeed treat AdSense as a long game: better content brings more visitors, more visitors bring more earnings, and the cycle compounds.

Track what works with free tools like Google Analytics and Search Console. Double down on the articles that get search traffic. Keep your site fast and mobile-friendly. And keep expectations honest — AdSense is real income for real content, not a shortcut to quick money.

AdSense FAQ

How long does AdSense approval take?

Most reviews take a few days to two weeks, though it can stretch to a month during busy periods. If you are rejected, Google tells you why — fix the issue and reapply rather than submitting the same site again.

How much traffic do I need before applying for AdSense?

Google sets no official traffic minimum. What matters is original, substantial content and a complete, policy-compliant site — small sites with genuinely useful articles do get approved.

Can I use AdSense and affiliate links on the same site?

Yes. Many publishers combine both: AdSense pays for ad views and clicks, while affiliate links earn commission on sales. This site does exactly that — the product boxes above are affiliate links.
