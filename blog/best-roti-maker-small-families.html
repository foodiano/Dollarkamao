How I Use It in Foodiano World (The Desi Twist)

On my YouTube channel Foodiano World, roti is the quiet hero of almost every recipe — and this is where the desi twist comes in. The twist I love: a press does not replace the tawa, it makes the tawa faster. Roll the atta dough into small balls, press each one gently between parchment sheets, and drop it straight onto the hot tawa. Because atta dough has gluten, buyers say it springs back a little more than corn masa, so press lightly and let the heat of the tawa finish the job — the roti puffs up just the same, and every piece comes out the same size, which is what a family dinner table really needs.

A tip from buyer reviews: always use parchment paper or a cut-open plastic bag between the dough and the plates, so the roti never sticks and the press stays clean. And the same press earns its keep beyond roti — buyers say it makes parathas, tortillas, quesadillas, empanadas and arepas, so one small tool covers the whole flatbread family. For a small family cooking desi food daily, buyers say that is the real value: one press, every bread, every night.

FAQ

Is a roti maker press worth it for a small family?

Buyers say yes, because rolling a perfect round roti by hand takes practice and time, and a press turns a ball of atta dough into an even round in seconds. For a small family that eats roti or chapati daily, buyers say the press saves the most time on busy weeknights, when four or five rotis need to be on the table fast. Buyers say a heavy cast iron press that costs around $30 and lasts for years is one of the cheapest upgrades a desi kitchen can make.

Can I use a tortilla press for roti and chapati dough?

Buyers say yes — the Victoria press is sold as a roti maker as well as a tortilla press, and buyers use it for rotis, chapatis, parathas, tortillas and quesadillas. Buyers say to place a small ball of atta dough between two sheets of parchment paper or plastic wrap so it does not stick, press gently, and then finish the roti on a hot tawa. Buyers say atta dough has gluten, so it springs back a little more than corn masa — a light press plus a quick finish on the tawa gives the softest result.

How do I stop a cast iron roti press from rusting?

Buyers say the number one rule is to dry it immediately and fully after every wash, because raw cast iron rusts fast when left wet. Buyers say to wash it with hot water and a stiff brush, dry it on a warm burner or with a towel right away, and wipe a very thin coat of cooking oil over the plates before putting it away. Buyers say never to soak it in the sink or leave it in a dish rack overnight, and never to put it in a dishwasher.
