FAQ

Is a 10-inch nonstick pan big enough for a small family?

Buyers say yes for most small families: a 10-inch pan comfortably handles two to four eggs, a family omelet, a batch of parathas or a pan of sautéed vegetables, while still fitting on a small stovetop and in a small cabinet. Buyers say the 12-inch version suits larger meal prep, but for everyday cooking for two to four people, the 10-inch size is the sweet spot — big enough for dinner, small enough to wash and store without a fight.

Can I use metal utensils on a nonstick frying pan?

Buyers say the OXO coating is described as resistant to scratches and able to handle metal utensils, but most buyers still reach for silicone, wood or plastic tools to keep the coating in top shape for years. Buyers say the safe rule is simple: metal will not ruin the pan on first use, but gentle utensils and a soft sponge make the nonstick surface last much longer — and that is what keeps eggs sliding off instead of sticking.

How long does a nonstick frying pan last, and when should I replace it?

Buyers say a well-cared-for nonstick pan lasts a few years of daily cooking, and the signs it is time to replace it are food starting to stick, visible scratches deep in the coating, or warping that makes the pan wobble on the burner. Buyers say hand-washing, medium heat instead of maximum heat, and storing the pan without stacking heavy pots on it are the three habits that stretch its life the longest. At around $36, buyers say, replacing a worn pan every few years is still far cheaper than buying a premium pan once.
